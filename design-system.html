Design proposal grounded in Orange’s existing content. Team members, reviews, insurers, coverage boundaries, and credentials appear only after verification. This guide is not a published patient website.

01 / Strategy

Build on the good work.

Keep the current draft’s warmth, human focus, and clear care process. Bring services, geographic fit, payment questions, and the phone number forward.

Help first

One clear next step: talk to the team. A readable telephone link stays close to every important decision.

Evidence earns trust

Use care-process facts now. Add real people, credentials, and reviews when their sources and permissions are established.

Calm, with purpose

Blue information surfaces, warm white space, simple layouts, and restrained orange action buttons.

Comfortable to read

18px body copy, generous line height, clear labels, large targets, and no moving content to chase.

02 / Color

A medical palette.
A human temperature.

Orange points to action. Blue provides calm. Navy carries the information. The lighter brand colors stay out of small text.

White on action orange: 5.28:1. Navy on white: 12.75:1. Navy on light blue: 7.07:1. Full palette, roles, and state colors in the specification.

Where does payment guidance belong?

07 / Homepage direction

Answer the questions
in the right order.

Bring practical decisions forward, retain the draft’s human tone, and make optional proof conditional on actual evidence.

  1. Header + heroWhat care is this, and how can I reach someone?
  2. Early evidenceWhat can the organization substantiate?
  3. ServicesDoes this match the care I’m looking for?
  4. Area + paymentIs care available where I live, and whom can I ask about costs?
  5. How care worksWhat happens after I get in touch?
  6. Why OrangeHow will my needs and preferences be understood?
  7. People + reviewsOptional, evidence-dependent reassurance.
  8. FAQs + contactResolve uncertainty and make the next step simple.
