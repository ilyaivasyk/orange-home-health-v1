# Orange Home Health Inc. — research and UX audit

Reviewed 20 September 2026. This is an expert UX review, not patient research or a formal accessibility certification. All six requested references, Orange’s original website, and the Uncoder draft were opened in a browser. Reference screenshots were inspected at a 1280 × 720 desktop viewport; text and accessibility trees informed the structural review. The draft’s computed typography and links were inspected. A requested mobile viewport override did not take effect, so no mobile pass/fail claims are made about the remote sites. Responsive specifications in the design system are recommendations.

Competitor statements below describe what their sites display, not independently verified clinical performance. No competitor credentials, service areas, ratings, people, photographs, or copy should migrate to Orange.

## 7. Orange’s original website: content worth retaining

The original [homepage](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/) establishes the business identity, home-health focus, contact details, and an ACHC logo. Its dominant masthead, scattered navigation, and duplicated action labels weaken the journey. Preserve the business content while replacing this presentation.

| Evidence | Source | Use in redesign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six service categories: skilled nursing, home health aide, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, medical social work | [Services](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/services/) | These six categories form the services hub. Do not expand “and more” into invented offerings. |
| Assessment followed by an individualized service plan | [Services](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/services/) | Retain a simple explanation of getting started. |
| Registered nurses/LVNs; communication with the primary physician; examples including medication supervision and wound care | [Skilled Nursing](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/skilled-nursing/) | Ground the nursing description and coordination component in these facts; clinical detail still needs current editorial review. |
| English, Spanish, Armenian, Russian, Tagalog, Farsi | [About](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/about/) | “Ask about support in your preferred language.” Listed staff languages do not establish 24-hour availability or full website translation. |
| Office: 13735 Victory Blvd, Suite 18, Van Nuys, CA 91401; phone 818-532-5353; email contact@orangehc.com | [Contact](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/contact/) | Centralize details in WordPress; label the office separately from home-visit coverage. |
| Fax 818-584-8822 | [Homepage](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/) and visible header | Keep in contact details; publish referral instructions only after routing is confirmed. |
| ACHC logo appears | [Homepage](https://orangehomehealthinc.com/) | Evidence that a logo was published, not independent confirmation of current status. Obtain certificate/directory evidence and permitted artwork before displaying a current accreditation claim. |

The original About page mentions around-the-clock personnel. That does not establish office hours, an emergency line, or a callback commitment. Confirm the operational meaning before creating a “24/7” component. No named employees, founding date, insurance list, verified review inventory, or exact home-visit coverage boundaries were established from the pages inspected.

## 8. Audit of the current Uncoder draft

Source: [Orange Home Health draft](https://orangehomehealthinc.uncoder.co/), inspected directly in the browser because the text retrieval tool could not load it.

### Keep

The calm white background, warm care-at-home imagery, sentence-case headings, simple FAQ disclosures, visible skip link, and restrained orange actions. The three-stage care explanation makes the process approachable. The two featured service panels and four additional service links already reflect the original service inventory. Preserve the language-support information with availability caveats.

### Improve

The hero conveys warmth but the healthcare category sits in a 13px uppercase eyebrow. Make “Home health care in Los Angeles” immediately readable at 16–18px; retain the current emotional headline or shorten it. Prefer text beside the photograph so contrast remains stable. If retaining the overlaid layout, test every crop and video frame rather than relying on a single screenshot.

Computed desktop sizes included 15px navigation/actions and 16px welcome copy. Increase navigation to 17px and body copy to 18px. Keep the original generous spacing but shorten repeated emotional headings. Name service-card headings by service before using supporting brand language.

Use one action name, “Talk to our team,” across the header, hero, and closing section. Show a telephone link in the header. Different service links currently lead to the same generic services page; give them meaningful anchors or unique destinations. Preserve the URLs of existing useful pages when implementing WordPress.

### Remove or replace

Remove repeated promises that add no practical information, tiny decorative eyebrow labels, and extra internal arrow flourishes. Replace production links that unexpectedly return to the old visual design when new templates are ready. Do not replace valid destinations with dead placeholder links.

The draft hero is explicitly illustrative in its alt text and its asset is named `hero-generated.jpg`. Use it only as a labeled concept image; it must not imply that these are actual Orange employees or patients. The large glossy SVG produced earlier is also a reconstruction, not an approved identity master. Retain Orange’s recognizable existing logo during this UX exercise; select approved header artwork during brand implementation.

### Missing

An early service-area answer; payment/eligibility guidance; a simple same-site enquiry route; identifiable real staff if supplied; source-linked reviews if supplied; accessible privacy and patient-information links; meaningful field/error/success states; clear distinction between office address and coverage. Lack of evidence is a reason to omit a claim, not to insert simulated badges.

### Reorganize

Move services ahead of the long welcome passage. Put geographic fit and payment questions immediately after services. Merge overlapping About and Approach messaging into a concise “Why Orange” section. Keep the three steps, then optional verified team/review material, FAQs, contact, and footer. This brings decisions forward while retaining the draft’s personality.

## 9. Synthesis: decisions, not copied layouts

| Decision for Orange | Reference insight | User benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early service-area help, initially a call/contact route | Interim; GrandCare | Reduces uncertainty before a visitor spends time on an enquiry. |
| Payment questions near services, with no unverified insurer logos | Beverly Hills; Danny’s; Miracle | Acknowledges a common barrier without promising coverage. |
| Plain service names and distinct detail destinations | Los Angeles Home Health; Miracle | Makes comparison possible without clinical expertise. |
| Optional real team and sourced reviews | Miracle; Danny’s | Replaces vague reassurance with accountable evidence. |
| Phone and one consistent contact CTA | Danny’s; Orange draft | Supports visitors who prefer conversation over forms. |
| Separate provider and careers routes | GrandCare; Interim | Keeps secondary audiences from interrupting the patient journey. |
| Warm photography, open layouts, readable type | Orange draft | Preserves the strongest existing work and reduces redesign churn. |

These are design hypotheses to validate with patients, family caregivers, and staff. Suggested tasks: find a service, establish geographic fit, locate the phone number, ask about payment, and explain the next step after contacting the agency. Observe completion and confusion before setting conversion targets.
